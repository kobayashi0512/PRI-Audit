# PRI-Audit journal positioning (post-audit, 2026-08-19)

## Frozen target and fallback sequence

1. **BMC Bioinformatics — primary target, Research Article.** The journal's
   scope explicitly covers novel computational algorithms, software, models,
   statistical methods and machine learning for biological data, including
   transcriptome analysis. This is a direct match for a falsification-first
   evaluation framework with a psoriasis use case. The full evidence chain
   (patient-boundary controls, null distributions, known-truth simulation and
   source manifests) can remain in the submission package instead of being
   cosmetically compressed.
2. **Bioinformatics Advances — first fallback, Original Article.** Its scope
   fits the study, but the current Original Article limit is eight pages with
   at most six figures and six tables. Choose it only after a deliberate,
   evidence-preserving condensation rather than deleting core reproducibility
   checks ad hoc.
3. **GigaScience — conditional alternative if the repository becomes exemplary.**
   Its evaluation emphasises reproducibility, usability and utility, which
   directly fits the raw hashes, one-command runner, output provenance and full
   null distributions.  This route requires an open-source license, immutable
   code/data identifiers, machine-readable supporting results and a genuinely
   independently runnable workflow; do not submit before these are ready.
4. **Journal of Biomedical Informatics — conditional, not default.** Its
   emphasis on general methodology and translational relevance is attractive,
   but it expects a conceptual contribution and a real-world biomedical
   problem evaluation, often with clinical professional involvement.  Choose
   it only if the general theory/decision rule and practical clinical-analytics
   implications are substantially strengthened; do not market an exploratory
   omics benchmark as a clinical decision-support system.

## Required submission framing

**Title logic:** “PRI-Audit: a falsification-first framework for testing
patient-level gain from high-resolution transcriptomic representations.”

**One-sentence claim:** High-resolution disease structure is not, by itself,
evidence of an incremental and reproducible patient-level prediction benefit;
PRI-Audit makes that claim testable through outer-fold representation fitting,
matched references, independent-unit inference and representation-specific
counterfactual nulls.

**Do not frame as:** a new best classifier, a clinical diagnostic tool, proof
that spatial biology is absent, or a causal study of psoriasis triggers.

## Evidence used for this positioning

* BMC Bioinformatics' current scope includes novel computational algorithms,
  software, models, statistical methods and machine learning for biological
  data, and explicitly lists transcriptome analysis. Its research-article
  criterion is a clear, sound research question with appropriate methodology
  and analysis.
* Bioinformatics Advances’ public scope includes algorithms, statistics,
  software, emerging technologies and disease/translational bioinformatics;
  its Original Article guidance specifies the eight-page / six-figure /
  six-table boundary.
* GigaScience states that reproducibility, usability and utility are central
  criteria and requires public data/code access and an OSI-approved software
  license for code central to conclusions.
* Journal of Biomedical Informatics describes itself as a methodology journal
  requiring a generalizable methodological contribution, state-of-the-art
  comparison and real-world biomedical relevance.

Before submission, recheck current fees, page limits, license options and
author guidance on the official journal websites; those policies can change.
